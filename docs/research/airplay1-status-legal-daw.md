## 1. Does current iOS/macOS still stream to a classic (`_raop._tcp`-only) receiver?

### Confirmed from sources

- **The iOS 18 scare and fix.** shairport-sync issue #1866 "[Problem]: iOS 18 beta 1 Breaks AirPlay 1 Support" (opened 2024-06-11): AirPlay 1 "connected, but playback failed. The device reported that it could not connect"; the log showed the client closing the session after OPTIONS. Real AirPlay-1-only hardware (1st-gen AirPort Express, a Yamaha receiver) failed too, so it was a sender bug. "This is fixed in iOS 18 beta 4" (2024-07-23); maintainer: "I can confirm this -- it's back in iPadOS Beta 4 too" (2024-07-24). https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/1866 **[C]**. Takeaway: Apple treated the classic-receiver regression as a bug and fixed it before release.
- **iOS 18.4.1:** issue #2023 (2025-05-12, iPhone 12, iOS 18.4.1): "If I run the classic version of the docker image, I get sound from Owntone and the iPhone." https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2023 **[C]**
- **macOS Tahoe 26.0.1 as sender:** issue #2085 (2025-10-02): the maintainer "tested on a MacBook Pro M1 with Tahoe 26.0.1 in classic mode, and it worked"; the reporter's failure was with the AirPlay 2 image and was closed as a local problem. https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2085 **[C]**
- **iOS/macOS 26.5:** issue #2203 (2026-05-23): maintainer Mike Brady "said Shairport Sync works with iOS 26.5 and macOS 26.5" and "AirPlay 2 requires a wider selection of accessible ports than classic AirPlay (aka AirPlay 1)". https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2203 **[C]**
- **Classic still in daily use in 2025–26:** issue titles from the tracker: "[Problem]: AirPlay 1 volume resets on each connection" (2025-11-29, https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2108), "shairport-sync failing to post pw=true in avahi for Airplay 1" (2026-03-06, https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2158) **[C]** (titles only, from the GitHub search API).
- **Music Assistant users fall back to classic:** music-assistant/support #6243 (2026-08-29): "forcing shairport-sync into classic AirPlay 1 mode (--service-type=classic, which also disables its nqptp) works reliably"; tradeoff: "the receiver can't join a Music Assistant sync group at all (no native multi-room sync in AirPlay 1)". https://github.com/music-assistant/support/issues/6243 **[C]**
- **iPadOS 26.5.2 / iOS 27.0 problems are AirPlay-2-mode problems:** issue #2238 (2026-07-17): AP2 session with no `shk` session key from Amazon Music; classic mode was neither tried nor implicated. https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2238 **[C]**
- **UxPlay (RSA-free legacy path) with iOS 26.6:** FDH2/UxPlay #547 (2026-08-02): "Full RAOP audio handshake succeeds (GET /info, POST /fp-setup x2, SETUP, RECORD)", "audio plays fine"; maintainer: "I can confirm that uxplay works with iOS 26.6 clients". https://github.com/FDH2/UxPlay/issues/547 **[C]**. UxPlay README: "While there is no guarantee that future iOS releases will keep supporting 'Legacy Protocol', iOS 17 continues support." https://github.com/FDH2/UxPlay **[C]**
- **Real AirPort Express still works:** Hackaday, 2025-11-24: the author "could readily stream audio from his iPhone and MacBooks with no compatibility issues whatsoever." https://hackaday.com/2025/11/24/the-airport-express-still-works-in-2025-thanks-to-apples-ongoing-support/ **[C]**
- **What classic lacks (shairport-sync AIRPLAY2.md):** "Classic Airplay offers only one format, ALAC/S16/44100/2"; AirPlay 2 adds synchronised multi-device audio, Home app, buffered mode with "a very short delay", 48 kHz/24-bit. https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/blob/master/AIRPLAY2.md **[C]**
- **Apple's multi-speaker doc** (support.apple.com/105068, published 2026-05-01): "Tap each speaker or TV that you want to play the current audio to"; "You can't use AirPlay to stream audio to multiple speakers from Control Center on a Mac." It does not mention AirPlay 1 at all. https://support.apple.com/en-us/105068 **[C]**. Third-party guides say AirPlay 1 devices "still work singly, but they can't be included in a multi-room set" (sealsystems.net, undated) **[C, secondary]**.
- shairport-sync README: AirPlay 1 is "the older, but still supported, AirPlay (aka 'AirPlay 1') protocol"; "there is no assurance that Shairport Sync will continue to work in future." https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync **[C]**

### Not found
- No evidence that iOS 17.x hid classic receivers from Control Center. The only "not showing" classic issue found is #1581 (2022-11-16) "only one shairport-sync (classic) instance is showing up in ios16", which is an mDNS/device-id collision topic, not a protocol drop. **[C, title only]**
- No pyatv / Home Assistant / Reddit threads about iOS 26 removing classic.

### Inferred
- The only measurable "degradation" for a classic receiver is the UI one: it is a single-select destination (no circle/group checkbox), cannot join a multi-room group, cannot be added to Home, and is pinned at 44.1 kHz/16-bit ALAC. For a DAW-input product none of that matters. **[I]**
- Apple fixing the iOS 18 regression, and a 2004 AirPort Express still streaming in late 2025, are the strongest signals that the classic path is being maintained rather than deprecated. **[I]**

## 2. Legal / IP: the RAOP private key and the AirPlay trademark

### Provenance (confirmed)
- Wikipedia (RAOP): the public key in iTunes "was extracted by Jon Lech Johansen" (VLC list, Dec 2008); "The RSA private key stored in the AirPort Express was extracted by James Laird, enabling simulation of an Airport Express" (VLC list, April 2011). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Audio_Output_Protocol **[C]**
- Habr, 2011-04-11: Laird, helping his girlfriend's AirPort Express, took the unit apart, read the ROM, reverse-engineered the firmware and extracted the private key; he released ShairPort under a free licence. https://habr.com/en/articles/117212/ **[C]**
- schrankmonster.de, 2011-04-19, quoting the announcement: "there's a private key hiding inside the ApEx"; the blogger adds it is "unsure if Apple will react by changing the encryption key in the future". https://www.schrankmonster.de/2011/04/19/shairport-someone-reversed-an-airport-express/ **[C]**
- Original shairport README: "Thanks also to Apple for obfuscating the private key in the ROM image" (Laird's announcement link, mafipulation.org/blagoblig/2011/04/08, is dead and the Wayback copy could not be fetched from this environment). https://github.com/abrasive/shairport **[C]**

### Who ships the key, and how they frame it (confirmed)
- shairport-sync embeds it (`rsa_apply()` in common.c; see issue #2115, 2025-12-19, "rsa_apply in common.c broken for CONFIG_MBEDTLS", https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/issues/2115). It is in **Debian main** (stable 4.3.7-1, testing/unstable 5.1~dev~git20260518-1, maintainer Chris Boot, in testing since at least 2017-08-06) https://tracker.debian.org/pkg/shairport-sync and **Fedora** (4.3.7-5.fc45, licence "LicenseRef-Callaway-MIT AND LicenseRef-Callaway-BSD AND GPL-3.0-or-later AND ISC") https://packages.fedoraproject.org/pkgs/shairport-sync/shairport-sync/ **[C]**. Neither the README nor TROUBLESHOOTING.md mentions the key or legal status. **[C]**
- shairplay (juhovh) takes the opposite line: "you need to have the airport.key file in your working directory"; "It is not included in the binary for possible legal reasons"; "meant for educational purposes only"; "It is the responsibility of the user to make sure all local laws are adhered to." https://github.com/juhovh/shairplay **[C]**
- New 2025–26 projects ship it without comment: AirPlayWin (MIT, C++23 Windows receiver, "legacy RSA-AES (et=0,1)", "handles Apple-Challenge, RSA-OAEP session-key recovery, AES-128-CBC audio") https://github.com/SunXiaomin916/AirPlayWin; ESPHome-AirPlay (GPL-3.0, credits shairport-sync for "RTSP protocol and authentication", no legal note) https://github.com/inventor7777/ESPHome-AirPlay **[C]**
- Projects that do **not** need the private key: **UxPlay** (GPL-3.0) has no Apple-Challenge/RSA code at all — it uses FairPlay `fp-setup` and pair-setup/pair-verify (its own legal exposure is FairPlay, not the RAOP key). **OwnTone** (GPL-2.0) and **pyatv** (MIT) are senders; OwnTone embeds only the RSA *public* key (`raop_rsa_pubkey`) to encrypt the AES key, pyatv has no RSA code at all. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FDH2/UxPlay/master/lib/raop_handlers.h , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/owntone/owntone-server/master/src/outputs/raop.c , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/postlund/pyatv/master/pyatv/support/rtsp.py **[C]**
- **Sonos and Roku do not use the key**: they are licensed AirPlay 2 implementations (Sonos announced AirPlay 2 "through a free software update in July" 2018, https://www.sonos.com/en-us/newsroom/meet-sonos-beam; Roku OS 9.4 added AirPlay 2 + HomeKit in Nov 2020). "AirPlay audio" is listed among "MFi licensed technologies"; the program costs "USD $99 ... per membership year" and royalties are "only available under NDA". https://mfi.apple.com/faqs **[C]**

### Apple's reactions (confirmed / not found)
- No lawsuit, DMCA notice or cease-and-desist against shairport, shairport-sync, UxPlay, OwnTone, AirPlayWin or the distributions packaging them was found. GitHub's public DMCA archive contains Apple notices for HomeKit specs (2014), iBoot (2018) and apps.apple.com assets (2025), none for AirPlay/RAOP. https://github.com/github/dmca **[C]** Absence of evidence in a 15-year window, not proof. **[I]**
- The one documented action is **App Store policy**: Airfoil Speakers Touch 3 (an iOS AirPlay *receiver*) was pulled on 2012-05-24; Rogue Amoeba: "we've implemented Airfoil Speakers Touch using only publicly-available APIs"; it returned 2012-06-06 without the receiving feature; Phil Schiller (2012-06-08): the app "accessed encrypted AirPlay audio streams without using approved APIs or a proper license". https://mjtsai.com/blog/2012/05/24/airfoil-speakers-touch-removed-from-the-app-store **[C]**. Rogue Amoeba's desktop receiver (Airfoil Satellite for Mac/Windows, "Receive audio ... on your iOS device or other computer") is still sold outside the App Store. https://rogueamoeba.com/airfoil/mac/ **[C]**

### Trademark (confirmed)
- Apple's "Guidelines for Using Apple Trademarks and Copyrights": developers may use an Apple word mark in a referential phrase such as "runs on," "for use with," "for," or "compatible with" provided the product "is in fact compatible with, or otherwise works with, the referenced Apple product", the mark is not part of the product name and is less prominent than it, and it "does not create a sense of endorsement, sponsorship, or false association with Apple". No logos; no variations; outside the US use "_____ is a trademark of Apple Inc., registered in the U.S. and other countries and regions." The page never mentions AirPlay, MFi or "Works with" badges. https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/guidelinesfor3rdparties.html **[C]**
- Apple's trademark list carries "AirPlay®" with generic term "software feature". https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/trademark/appletmlist.html **[C]**
- The "Works with Apple AirPlay" badge is the MFi badge: licensees "include the correct MFi badge on the product packaging ... once it has completed the necessary program requirements" (MFi FAQ); licensee manuals define it as "designed to work specifically with the technology identified in the badge and ... certified by the developer to meet Apple performance standards" (Denon AVC-X3800H manual, https://manuals.denon.com/AVCX3800H/EU/PL/GFNFSYbxvayfun.php). **[C]**

### Inferred for the human's decision
- Descriptive text ("Tape In is compatible with AirPlay" + the attribution line) fits the guidelines; the badge, the AirPlay icon, and "AirPlay" in the product name do not. **[I]**
- The key is identical in every project; the legal question is the same one Debian, Fedora, Arch, Homebrew and OpenWrt answered yes to by packaging shairport-sync. The differentiator for a *commercial-looking* product is Schiller's 2012 phrase "without ... a proper license" — Apple's leverage was distribution channel, not litigation. An AGPL open-source build distributed outside the App Store is in the same position as shairport-sync; an AU/VST3 plugin cannot go through the Mac App Store anyway. **[I]**

## 3. Can the RSA step (Apple-Challenge / `a=rsaaeskey`) be avoided?

### Confirmed
- shairport-sync receiver code: `apple_challenge()` is called on every RTSP request; if the header is absent it simply returns (no error). In ANNOUNCE, if neither `a=aesiv` nor `a=rsaaeskey` is present the session is accepted unencrypted (`conn->stream.encrypted = 0`); if only one is present → 456. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/master/rtsp.c (lines ~3484–3490, ~3648) **[C]**
- The only direct Apple-sender test of `et=0` found (go-airplay notes, iTunes 11.1.3 era, c. 2013): "If the encryption record et= was omitted, or had value et=0 (no encryption), the iPhone/iPad did not see it, but iTunes saw it as a speaker" and iTunes then sent no Apple-Challenge; with `et=0,1` "both iTunes and the iOS devices saw it. An Apple-Challenge was seen from both iTunes and iOS." https://github.com/joelgibson/go-airplay/blob/master/README.md **[C, dated]**
- airplay2-receiver #12 (2020): iTunes for Windows "sends an OPTION request with an 'Apple-Challenge'"; "Apple-Challenge refers to RSA Authentication, which is not allowed for AP2"; "RSA is only working when using the _raop._tcp bonjour service." https://github.com/openairplay/airplay2-receiver/issues/12 **[C]**
- Non-Apple senders do not need RSA: pyatv's ANNOUNCE has only `a=rtpmap`/`a=fmtp` lines and a comment "Used to signal that traffic is to be unencrypted"; OwnTone adds Apple-Challenge "only if session is encrypted" and sets `encrypt` only when the receiver's TXT has `ek=1` or it is an AirPort Express; shairplay-rust PR #44 (2026-07) defaulted to `et=0` because PipeWire "selects the highest offered et" and its RSA path is broken. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/postlund/pyatv/master/pyatv/support/rtsp.py , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/owntone/owntone-server/master/src/outputs/raop.c (lines ~1595–1611, ~4416), https://github.com/metaneutrons/shairplay-rust/pull/44 **[C]**
- The RSA-free path that modern iOS *does* use: UxPlay advertises `et=0,3,5` (None, FairPlay, FairPlay SAPv2.5), `cn=0,1,2,3`, features `0x5A7FFEE6` "with bit 27 ('supports legacy pairing') ON", answers `fp-setup` ("Only type 0x03 can be handled using publically-available information") and pair-setup/pair-verify (SRP, X25519/Ed25519); it has no Apple-Challenge code, and iOS 26.6 streams audio to it (issue #547). https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FDH2/UxPlay/master/lib/dnssdint.h , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FDH2/UxPlay/master/lib/raop_handlers.h **[C]**
- pyatv's protocol table: `et` "0=unencrypted, 1=RSA (AirPort Express), 3=FairPlay, 4=MFiSAP, 5=FairPlay SAPv2.5". https://pyatv.dev/documentation/protocols/ **[C]**

### Inferred
- Two viable designs: (A) classic `et=0,1` + ship the key (shairport-sync/auxCord-style; iOS sends Apple-Challenge and expects a valid Apple-Response); (B) `et=0,3,5` + FairPlay + legacy pairing (UxPlay-style; no RSA key but a reverse-engineered FairPlay v3 handshake, which is DRM code and a larger surface). An `et=0`-only receiver is, on the only evidence available, invisible to iOS. **[I]**
- That evidence is 13 years old. Before deciding, run the experiment: register `_raop._tcp` with `et=0` only (and separately `et=0,1` with no Apple-Response) from a throwaway receiver and watch whether an iOS 26 device lists it and what it sends after OPTIONS. Cost: under an hour with `dns-sd -R` plus a tiny RTSP responder. **[I]**

## 4. macOS Local Network privacy, plugins inside DAWs, Logic's hosting model, Application Firewall

### Confirmed — TN3179 "Understanding local network privacy" (Apple, current revision mentions macOS 27.2)
https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3179-understanding-local-network-privacy
- Operations table: outgoing TCP connection — **yes**; "Listening for and accepting incoming TCP connections: **no**"; sending UDP unicast/multicast/broadcast — **yes**; connecting a UDP socket — yes; "Receiving an incoming UDP unicast: **no**"; receiving incoming UDP multicast/broadcast — yes. "A local network address is any address on a local network ... all multicast addresses ... and the IPv4 broadcast address are local network addresses."
- "All Bonjour operations require local network access" (register, browse, resolve). High-level services (AirPlay, DeviceDiscoveryUI, AccessorySetupKit) "don't require local network access". Resolving a `.local` name requires it.
- Attribution: "if your app spawns a helper tool and the helper tool performs a local network operation, macOS considers the app to be the responsible code." The choice is recorded "for the whole app, not just that specific helper tool".
- Automatically allowed: "Any daemon started by launchd; Any program running as root; Command-line tools run from Terminal or over SSH, including any child processes they spawn." "The exception for launchd daemons doesn't apply to launchd agents" — agents not installed via SMAppService should set `AssociatedBundleIdentifiers` in the launchd plist.
- Keys: "add the NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription property to its Info.plist"; NSBonjourServices lists service types "in your app's Info.plist". For app extensions: "add the NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription and NSBonjourServices properties to the app's Info.plist, not to the app extension's." A standalone executable that needs the key must embed an Info.plist section in the binary. XPC services and plug-ins are not mentioned.
- "Local network privacy uses your main executable UUID as part of its implementation."
- Known bug: "macOS fails to display the local network alert when a process with a very short lifespan performs a local network operation (FB16131937)."
- Reset: "These buttons were introduced in macOS 27.2. On earlier versions of macOS there's no way to reset your program's Local Network privilege (FB14944392)." Test in a VM or a fresh user account.
- Eclectic Light (2026-01-18, tested on macOS 26.2): it is implemented by the Network Extension framework, "TCC knows nothing about local network privacy"; rules key on `matchSigningIdentifier = org.videolan.vlc`; "the system implements these TCP and UDP checks deep in the networking stack, and thus they apply to all networking APIs." https://eclecticlight.co/2026/01/18/last-week-on-my-mac-local-network-privacy-revealed/ **[C]**
- Michael Tsai's running thread (2024-10-02, updated to 2025-10-21): Quinn: "Terminal is considered the responsible code"; "local network privacy does not apply to code running as root"; launchd agents prompt; on macOS the common accidental trigger is `NSHost`/`-[NSProcessInfo hostName]`. https://mjtsai.com/blog/2024/10/02/local-network-privacy-on-sequoia/ **[C]**

### Confirmed — plugins inside DAWs
- Sound on Digital (Mix to Mobile, an AU/VST plugin that talks to a phone): "make sure that the host application has access to the Local Network." https://soundondigital.com/support/allow-local-network-access-on-macos-sequoia/ **[C]**
- Native Instruments / inMusic support (macOS 15): enable the Privacy & Security > Local Network entries for the audio software **and the DAW**. https://inmusicsupport.freshdesk.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000879992-how-to-grant-audio-apps-access-to-your-local-network-on-macos-15-sequoia **[C]**

### Existing plugins that do networking inside the DAW

### Inferred — validation of the hypothesis
- A classic RAOP receiver must (a) register `_raop._tcp` (Bonjour → permission required), (b) listen on TCP/UDP (no permission required), and (c) send UDP timing requests to the sender's timing port (unicast to a local address → permission required). So a receiver inside the DAW process will trigger the prompt for the DAW, the prompt will say "Logic Pro / Live would like to find and connect to devices on your local network" with the DAW's own usage string (or none), the plugin's Info.plist keys are ignored, and on Apple-Silicon Logic a sandbox-safe AU may find UDP denied outright. This confirms "the plugin must not open sockets". **[I]**
- Corollary that changes the design: TN3179's responsible-code rule means **the helper must not be spawned by the plugin** (otherwise the DAW is the responsible code again). Launch it as a user-started app, a login item, or a launchd agent carrying `AssociatedBundleIdentifiers`, and have the plugin only rendezvous with it (shared memory + a loopback or Mach/XPC control channel; loopback traffic is not "local network" per TN3179's address definition). The helper carries `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription` and `NSBonjourServices` (`_raop._tcp`). **[I]**
- Quinn's Aug-2025 answer shows that even a `LocalOnly` Bonjour registration prompts, so do not use Bonjour for plugin↔helper discovery. **[I]**
- Firewall: incoming-connection prompts are attributed to the listening executable; with the receiver in a separately signed Developer-ID app, the user sees one prompt for "Tape In" rather than one for Logic/AUHostingService. **[I]**

## 7. Multiple simultaneous senders to one receiver

### Confirmed
- Protocol: SETUP's Transport reply carries per-session `server_port`, `control_port`, `timing_port` ("server_port=53561;control_port=63379;timing_port=50607", `Session: 1`); each sender has its own RTSP TCP connection. https://openairplay.github.io/airplay-spec/audio/rtsp_requests/setup.html **[C]**
- shairport-sync (classic): `allow_session_interruption = "no"` default — "(Classic AirPlay only) Set to 'yes' to allow another device to interrupt Shairport Sync while it's playing"; `session_timeout = 60`. In code, a second connection while a principal connection exists and interruption is disallowed is simply closed ("session interruption not allowed"); an ANNOUNCE that cannot take the principal slot gets **453**; AirPlay 2 SETUP likewise 453. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/master/scripts/shairport-sync.conf , rtsp.c lines ~3604, ~4486 **[C]**
- AirPlayWin: "One active sender by default; a second ANNOUNCE receives RTSP 453", with an optional preemption policy. UxPlay: exclusive by default, `-nohold` lets a new client take over, `-reset n`. ESPHome-AirPlay: "One receiver per device ... Your Apple device may allow you to AirPlay to multiple at once, but performance is not guaranteed." https://github.com/SunXiaomin916/AirPlayWin , https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/resolute/man1/uxplay.1.html , https://github.com/inventor7777/ESPHome-AirPlay **[C]**
- auxCord: one instance accepts many senders — "You can connect multiple devices at the same time - up to 100" with per-device volume/solo/mute (Production Expert, 2022-12-16); the AlpHorn announcement (2021-12-21) already said "Individual connected devices can be muted or soloed." **[C]**
- Sender-side: pyatv allows one stream per client ("second call raises InvalidStateError"). **[C]**

### Inferred
- There is no protocol-level limit and no iOS-side coordination: iOS knows nothing about other senders; the receiver decides. The single-sender limit in open-source receivers is a design choice tied to one output clock. Tape In can accept N sessions by allocating a UDP port triplet, an AES key, a jitter buffer and a per-sender clock model per session, then mixing (each sender's timing is independent, so each session needs its own sync/resampling). What iOS shows when a receiver answers 453 is not documented; shairport-sync users see "Unable to connect"-type messages in that situation. **[I]**
- Open question to test: whether iOS refuses to select a receiver whose `_raop._tcp` TXT it has cached as "busy" (there is no such TXT field; `sf`/`flags` bits exist but their semantics are unpublished). **[I]**

## 8. AirPlay-1 latency: sender-imposed, and what a receiver can do

### Confirmed
- shairport-sync README 3.3.8: "The latency to be used is specified by the audio source when it negotiates with Shairport Sync. Most sources set a latency of two seconds. Recent versions of iTunes and forkedDaapd use a latency of just over 2.25 seconds. A latency of this length allows AirPlay players to correct for network delays, processing time variations and so on." Current README: "Classic AirPlay sources set a latency of around 2.0 to 2.25 seconds. AirPlay 2 can use shorter latencies, around half a second." (that is AirPlay 2 *buffered* mode; AIRPLAY2.md says AP2 realtime is "similar to the older Classic AirPlay format"). https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/3.3.8/README.md , https://github.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync **[C]**
- Mechanism (rtp.c): the sender's sync packet carries `rtp_timestamp_less_latency` (bytes 4–7, "the timestamp of the frame that should be playing" now) and `sync_rtp_timestamp` (bytes 16–19); latency `la = sync_rtp_timestamp - rtp_timestamp_less_latency`, plus an 11 025-frame fixed delay depending on a flags field ("iTunes set this field to 7 and AirPlay sets it to 4"), clamped to the sender's `a=min-latency`/`a=max-latency` from ANNOUNCE; "Sigh, it would be nice to have a published protocol...". A user override exists (`-L/--latency`, `config.userSuppliedLatency`; config key `latencies.default` is marked "Deprecated!"), and `-A/-i/--forkedDaapdLatency` are "deprecated and ignored. This setting is now automatically received from the AirPlay device." https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikebrady/shairport-sync/master/rtp.c (≈lines 395–420), shairport.c (≈450, 503–515, 1166) **[C]**
- Receiver's `Audio-Latency` in the RECORD reply is "for telling the client what the absolute minimum latency actually is, and when the client specifies a latency, it should be added to this figure. Thus, [the old version of] AirPlay's latency figure of 77175, when added to 11025 gives you exactly 88200, and iTunes' latency figure of 88553, when added to 11025 gives you 99578". shairport-sync answers 11025; AP2 mode answers 0; AirPlayWin answers 11025; the spec example shows an AirPort Express answering 2205. rtsp.c ≈lines 924–933; https://openairplay.github.io/airplay-spec/audio/rtsp_requests/record.html **[C]**
- iOS ANNOUNCE examples carry `a=min-latency:11025` (AAC audio) and `a=min-latency:4410` (AAC-ELD mirroring audio). https://openairplay.github.io/airplay-spec/audio/rtsp_requests/announce.html **[C]**
- UxPlay: "there is then a 2 second latency imposed by iOS" in audio-only mode; its `-al` option "specifies an audio latency ... that is reported to the client ... Default is 0.25 sec ... (However, the client appears to ignore this reported latency, so this option seems non-functional.)" https://github.com/FDH2/UxPlay **[C]**
- Sender-side knobs exist only in non-Apple senders: libraop `-l <latency>` in frames; OwnTone `RAOP_AUDIO_LATENCY_MS 250`; PipeWire's RAOP sink has a `latency` setting (an Arch user reports a 2000 ms default for a Bose bar). https://docs.pipewire.org/page_module_raop_discover.html , https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?id=250086 **[C]**
- `audio_backend_latency_offset_in_seconds` "is added to the latency requested by the player to delay or advance the output by a fixed amount" (sample config); AdjustingSync.md: "Latency adjustments should be small, not more than about ± 250 milliseconds." **[C]**
- DAW-oriented tools: auxCord makes no latency claim; the reviewer: "The latency is high" (Production Expert, 2022-12-16). Rogue Amoeba historically told reviewers the delay "is built into the Airport express and there is no way around it" (MacSparky, c. 2007). https://www.macsparky.com/?p=1355 **[C, secondary]**

### Inferred
- The ~2 s is entirely the sender's choice and is not negotiable over RTSP: `Audio-Latency` can only *add* delay (it tells the sender how much longer to delay local video), `a=min-latency` is the sender's own floor, and iOS ignores receiver-reported latency (UxPlay). **[I]**
- Because the sync packet's two timestamps differ by the latency, the sender is transmitting audio ~2 s *before* it is due to play; the receiver holds it. A receiver can therefore play early: shairport-sync's `-L` override and its ±250 ms offset prove the knob exists; the practical floor is network jitter + retransmission window + decode + output buffer (tens of ms on a good Wi-Fi link). The costs are lost A/V sync with the sender's screen, reduced tolerance for Wi-Fi hiccups (retransmit requests have less time), and odd behaviour on FLUSH/seek (audio already received past the flush point must be discarded). For a DAW-input product this "low-latency monitoring" mode is the main differentiator available over auxCord, and it should be a user-selectable buffer length (e.g. 100 ms / 500 ms / "sender sync"). **[I]**
- Why 11025: 0.25 s is the AirPort Express's own hardware buffer; it is folded into the 88 200 total, so a software receiver reporting 11025 while actually adding less latency already plays "early" relative to an AirPort Express. **[I]**
